Assumptions

  • Waves: deep water; ρsea = 1025 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s². Regular mode = one sinusoid. Irregular mode = three sinusoids (amplitudes 0.85a, 0.35a, 0.15a; frequencies ×1, ×1.5, ×2.33) like a swell spectrum. The buoy does not disturb the wave field.
  • Buoy (Body 1): a ring-shaped float that slides on the fixed central shaft and moves only in heave. Default D = 20 m with a 1.5 m hole radius; moving assembly mass defaults to 500 kg (adjustable from 250 to 3,000 kg). To preserve a comparable heave response across this mass range, the model scales its effective waterplane area in proportion to mass. At D = 20 m, the equilibrium draft therefore remains about 0.95 m. This is a normalized engineering model: the drawn 20 m shell is not a literal displacement calculation for a 500 kg structure.
  • Fixed structure (Body 2): a 48 m shaft on a seabed gravity anchor (32 m depth) carrying the dynamo station. It is rigid and does not move.
  • PTO: a rack hanging from the top plate meshes with a pinion in the station (default r = 1.20 m), followed by a multi-stage gearbox (default N = 190:1). A mechanical motion rectifier makes the generator always turn the same way: ωg = N|v|/r.
  • PTO force: linear damping F = c·v. The damping slider sets a reference coefficient cref; the applied coefficient is c = cref × (m / 300,000 kg), so PTO damping scales with moving mass along with the effective waterplane area. The displayed slider value is the resulting effective coefficient. When Pel would exceed the rated capacity, the force is reduced so it never does.
  • Generator (assumed Stamford-type brushless synchronous): 4 poles, rated 500 kW at 1500 rpm, 690 V line-to-line, power factor 0.9, constant excitation (voltage ∝ speed), efficiency 92 % constant. Readings are generator-side AC with variable frequency; the grid converter is not modelled.
  • Rotor and gearbox inertia are ignored: generator speed follows the buoy velocity instantly.
  • Visual only: rotor animation is slowed ×20; gear teeth are not to scale.
  • Results are predictions under idealised assumptions, not guaranteed real-ocean performance. The 500 kW generator rating is a ceiling, not a continuous output; with a 250–3,000 kg moving mass, predicted output will generally be far below that ceiling.
